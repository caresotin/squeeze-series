## 1. What LinkedIn is in 2026: free vs paid, in one view

Let's clear up the confusion first: in China, LinkedIn now has exactly one version.

In August 2023, the mainland-facing app (领英职场, "InCareer") was shut down for good. That door is closed. The only way in now is the international site, linkedin.com — and from mainland China it doesn't load on a plain connection. You need a stable, fixed network setup to reach it. That isn't a trick you can work around; it's a precondition. Accept it before reading further.

The account itself is free, but "free" and "usable" are different things. A free account can add people, send messages, and post updates normally — with three hard limits: a daily cap on how many profiles you can view, a monthly cap on searches, and a weekly cap on connection requests. None of these are posted anywhere in front of you; you find out when a warning pops up mid-action.

Above the free tier sit the paid products. Sales Navigator is the one export sellers actually use, and its core value comes down to two things: advanced filters for finding the right people, and 50 InMail credits a month to message them directly. There's also Premium Career, which is built for job hunting — for customer development it's basically useless. Don't buy the wrong one.

One line to hold on to: the free plan is your base — a complete profile and steady content will already get you moving. Sales Navigator is the paid upgrade that turns "needle in a haystack" into "following a map." It earns its keep once your volume is real.

## 2. Registration and profile: first impressions decide who accepts you

Registration has no real barrier — a +86 phone number or an email address both work. What separates people is the profile, because in export sales nobody adds you without clicking your page first for two seconds. That's the whole audition.

**Registration SOP**

1. Open linkedin.com in an environment that can reach the international site reliably. Register with an email — Gmail or a company address, never a throwaway inbox.
2. Fill in name, title, and company with your real information for now. Polish comes later.
3. After verifying your email, don't rush to add people. Finish the profile first, then move.
4. Turn on two-step verification (Settings → Sign in & security → Two-step verification). Same logic as WhatsApp: skip it and you're effectively leaving the door open.

**The profile four-piece set — two hours, once**

**First, the photo.** White background, face clearly visible, normal lighting. No scenery shots, no logos. Overseas buyers judge "is this person legit?" from that picture, and the weight is higher than you'd think.

**Second, the headline.** Don't write "Sales Manager at XX Company." The headline is the most-scanned field in free-tier search, so write it as a combination of product keywords + who you serve + region. Example:

> Baby Diapers Manufacturer | OEM/ODM for Amazon & Distributors | Export to LATAM

Now a customer searching "diapers manufacturer" finds you. That's the whole game.

**Third, the About section.** Write three paragraphs: what you make, who you serve, and why someone should pick you. Do not write "we are a professional company." Write specific products, certifications, capacity, and lead times — enough for a customer to decide in 30 seconds whether you're the supplier they're looking for.

**Fourth, experience and skills.** Write out 3–5 key roles properly, and fill the skills section with product-related keywords. These are weighted terms in search.

Plainly: your profile is not a résumé. It's a landing page. When a customer lands on it, it has 10 seconds to answer "who are you, what do you sell, why you."

## 3. The 5 mistakes every new user makes

Almost every LinkedIn beginner trips on these. Knowing them in advance saves you half the detour.

**Mistake 1: mass-adding on day one**

New accounts are watched the hardest. Adding dozens of people and blasting messages on your first day is the fastest way to hand the account back to LinkedIn. For the first two weeks, keep your adding pace slow — concrete numbers in section 4.

**Mistake 2: sending the default invite with no note**

A stranger receives a blank "I'd like to add you to my professional network," opens your profile, finds a competitor, and ignores it. Write your reason in the note. A clear note multiplies your acceptance rate several times over.

**Mistake 3: leading with ads and links**

Sliding a price list and a website link into the chat right after connecting is the fastest way to get blocked. LinkedIn has explicit rules on commercial behavior. Build the relationship first; talk business after.

**Mistake 4: third-party automation tools**

LinkedIn's detection of automation is mature in 2026. Hanging a tool on a new account is self-sabotage. Third-party bulk sending and batch adding are among the most common causes of bans. Manual work is slower — and your account survives longer.

**Mistake 5: constantly switching login environments**

Office computer today, a new proxy tomorrow, phone the day after. Frequent logins from new locations trigger identity verification and even temporary restrictions. Settle on 1–2 devices and networks and stick with them.

All five share one root cause: don't give the system reason to think a real person isn't behind the account. Operating at a normal human pace beats any clever trick.

## 4. Connection requests have a hidden cap — mind your pace

LinkedIn has never published an official "how many people can you add per week" number, but by 2026 the community's measured figures have converged hard.

**Current practical limits (experience values, not official)**

- Mature accounts: 100–200 requests per week — most accounts hit a real ceiling around 100
- Accounts in their first 3–6 months: 40–75 per week, 15–20 per day
- When you trip it, you'll see: "You've reached the weekly invitation limit"

In other words, the dream of adding several hundred people a day died somewhere before 2026. The cap is fixed — but your acceptance rate isn't. A clear note, some interaction before the request, and the right target selection will do more than raw volume ever did.

One detail worth knowing: free accounts get 200 characters for the connection note; paid accounts get 300. "Name + why you + one line of value" fits comfortably in 200.

Keep this in mind: adding people is screening, not assembly-line work. Fifteen to twenty hand-picked targets a day beats fifty blind adds every time.

## 5. SSI: what it is and how to raise it

LinkedIn runs an internal score called SSI — the Social Selling Index. It runs 0–100, updates daily, and is free to view. It doesn't directly decide whether you get banned, but it reflects how healthy and active your account looks, and it influences how much reach your content gets.

**SSI's four pillars**

1. Establish a professional brand — profile completeness, photo, headline, content quality
2. Find the right people — active searching, adding target customers
3. Engage with insights — commenting, liking, sharing other people's content
4. Build relationships — replying to messages, maintaining your network

Reading the score: 70+ is good, 40–70 is average, below 40 basically means you're not operating.

**How to raise it — two things, that's it**

First, spend a fixed 15 minutes a day doing normal-human things: scroll the feed, comment on a few industry posts, post one update. Second, stop treating the account like a tool. Run it like a real person would.

Going from 30 to 45 isn't hard. Consistency is the hard part. Keep posting and engaging for three months straight and the score rises on its own.

## 6. Is the free plan enough? The four walls

The free plan isn't useless — but it has four very real 2026 limits you should know before you plan customer development around it.

**The free plan's four walls**

| Limit | Free plan | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Profile views | ~80 per day | Trip it and you get restricted — hard metric |
| Searches | ~250–350 per month | Triggers the "commercial use limit" notice |
| Search results per page | 1,000 max | Sales Navigator: ~2,500 |
| Who viewed you | Last 5 profiles only | The full list is a paid feature |

Plus the note-length difference from section 4: 200 characters free, 300 paid.

So the judgment call is simple. If you're looking at 30–50 targets a day and adding people by hand, the free plan is fully sufficient. If you're screening hundreds a day and batch-filtering, the free plan will strangle you — that's the moment Sales Navigator earns its keep.

## 7. Is Sales Navigator worth it? Features and real prices

Sales Navigator is LinkedIn's sales edition, and its core function fits in one sentence: it turns "searching for people" into "screening people."

**What it actually adds**

- 40–50 advanced filters: industry, company size, seniority level, tenure, headquarters region, whether a company uses a certain type of technology, and more
- Saved searches with real-time alerts: the moment a new lead matches your criteria, you get notified
- Lead lists and account lists: group your targets and give follow-up a proper handle
- 50 InMail credits per month — direct messages to people who aren't your connections (every tier gets exactly 50)
- 2,500 results per page, more than double the free plan

**Real prices, 2026**

| Tier | Monthly | Annual (per-month equivalent) |
|---|---|---|
| Core | ~$119.99 | ~$89.99–99.99 |
| Advanced | ~$159.99 | ~$139.99–149.99 |
| Advanced Plus | Custom; ~$1,600+/seat/year | — |

A few things to watch: there's a 30-day free trial — cancel before it renews if you're not staying. Prices vary by region and promotion, so the official site's live quote wins. And every tier gets 50 InMail credits; upgrading doesn't add to your messaging allowance.

Worth it or not, run the numbers: the free plan's ~80 profile views a day works out to ~2,400 a month — that's basically your entire lead capacity. Sales Navigator's filters make the same time produce several times more qualified leads, and the saved-search alerts mean someone is watching for new customers on your behalf. For anyone running B2B steadily, that money usually comes back.

## 8. Precision search: pulling your target customer out of a billion people

This is the most valuable section in the whole guide. LinkedIn has a billion users. Finding "the people who would buy your product" is a search-composition problem — solve it and the platform does the rest.

**Step 1: define your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) first**

Before you touch the keyboard, answer three questions: what industry are your target customers in? How big are their companies? Who at that company actually makes the purchasing decision?

Example: you make diapers and you want South American distributors. Your ICP is: Brazil, industry = baby products, company size 11–50, title = Purchasing Manager / Founder.

**Step 2: stack filters — don't rely on keywords alone**

In Sales Navigator, type an industry term into the search box (e.g. "diaper distributor"), then stack filters on top: region Brazil, company size 11–200, seniority Owner/Manager, tenure 1+ years. Every filter you add tightens the result set.

**Step 3: learn Boolean syntax**

The search box supports AND / OR / NOT and parentheses. For example:

> diaper AND (distributor OR importer OR wholesaler) NOT manufacturing

That one query catches three customer segments at once and excludes your competitors. Good news: the free plan supports Boolean too, so you can practice without paying a cent.

**Step 4: save the search and let it watch for you**

Once you have a result set you're happy with, save the search and turn on new-lead alerts. From then on, open Sales Navigator daily and the platform tells you: "3 new matches today."

One last note: don't add the whole result set in one go. Browse it, drop the obviously wrong ones, then add in batches — and keep pace with the limits from section 4.

## 9. InMail done right: don't waste your 50 monthly credits

InMail is Sales Navigator's allowance for messaging people who aren't your connections — 50 credits a month. Used well, it's precision outreach. Used badly, it's spam.

**Three rules**

**First, InMail is not a broadcast tool.** Spend the 50 credits on high-value targets. Twenty hand-picked messages beat fifty template blasts.

**Second, credits come back.** If the recipient replies within 90 days, the credit is refunded. So the more your message reads like a person wrote it, the higher the reply rate — and the longer your 50 credits last.

**Third, read their profile before you write.** Mention their company, something they've been doing lately, their actual business. "Saw you're expanding into South America — we do OEM diapers" lands completely differently from "We are a professional diaper manufacturer, welcome to inquire." The first gets replies. The second gets archived.

**A free alternative**

If you don't want to pay, work the connect-plus-note route all the way first: once someone accepts, messaging them costs nothing and has no hard cap (just don't go overboard day to day). InMail exists to reach people who haven't accepted you — it's a supplement, not a requirement.

Remember what InMail is for: knocking on the door, not closing the deal. The only goal is to get the first sentence going.

## 10. Account restricted or banned? Triage first, then fix

Account problems feel like an emergency, but don't panic — figure out which case you're in and handle it in order.

**Case 1: temporary restriction (the most common one)**

Symptoms: searches get limited, you hit the "weekly invitation limit" notice, or login asks for identity verification. Cause: usually too much activity in a short window, or a new device and network.

Fix: stop everything. Spend 1–3 days just browsing normally and replying to messages. Most LinkedIn restrictions are temporary, and going quiet usually clears them.

**Case 2: identity verification requested**

Symptoms: login asks you to upload an ID or verify your email/phone. Cause: cross-location login or a risk-control spot check.

Fix: submit your real documents and contact details as prompted. Review typically takes a few days. Don't skip this step — the account stays locked until you do it.

**Case 3: account restricted**

Symptoms: features cut across the board, sometimes with a message that the account has been disabled. Cause: serious violations — automation tools, multiple reports from other users, bulk marketing.

Fix: file an official appeal and honestly describe what you use the account for. Appeals go through human review; an honest, factual account improves your odds, but there are no guarantees.

**The recovery period matters most**

A freshly restored account is watched for 1–2 weeks. Week one: keep adds to 5–10 a day and do nothing but normal browsing and interaction. No jumping straight back to heavy activity — treat it like recovering from a real illness: rest for a week, then ramp up gradually.

To put it plainly: LinkedIn's 2026 risk control isn't hunting for "you marketed." It's asking "does this look like a normal person using the platform?" Operate like a human and your account stays stable.

## 11. Advanced play: content that makes customers come to you

Adding people and sending messages is outbound. Content is what makes customers show up on their own. You need both legs — that's when acquisition gets efficient.

**The personal-account content trio**

**First, post 2–3 industry pieces a week.** Nobody needs essays. Product explainers, industry observations, factory day-to-day — all fine. What matters is that there's a point of view and real detail. Do not repost your company website's press release.

**Second, turn on Creator Mode.** On your personal profile, it gets your content more reach and lets you add topic tags. It's free advertising for yourself.

**Third, bank your best posts into articles or a Newsletter.** LinkedIn supports long-form articles and newsletter subscriptions, and one article the industry actually discusses can pull in more inquiries than 50 InMails.

**One principle for topic selection**

Write about what your target customers worry about — not about how great your company is. "3 certification traps when exporting diapers to South America" gets saved and shared; "Our company passed ISO certification" gets scrolled past.

Volume doesn't matter. Consistency does. Two posts a week, three months straight — the results speak for themselves.

## 12. Plugging LinkedIn into your full acquisition pipeline

At this point LinkedIn stops being a standalone platform and becomes one stage of your whole acquisition chain.

**Front end: LinkedIn finds people and builds trust**

Sales Navigator digs out the decision-makers → connect or InMail makes first contact → content keeps you visible. The output of this stage is "people willing to talk to you" — not orders.

**Middle: move the relationship to WhatsApp and email**

The moment a conversation on LinkedIn shows real intent, move it to WhatsApp (the Squeeze WhatsApp guide has the full playbook) or email. LinkedIn is a poor place to negotiate quote details, and its chat history is hard to manage. WhatsApp is where deals actually close.

**Back end: manage follow-up with Gmail**

Tag every target — new lead / connected / quoted / closed — and let Gmail filters plus labels sort them automatically. Don't let leads scatter across three platforms where you can't find them.

**Last: get paid through Wise or PayPal, with chat records as proof**

Keep quotes, samples, and order confirmations traced in WhatsApp. Receive payments via Wise's multi-currency account or PayPal (rate and fee comparison is in the series' two payment guides). When there's a dispute, you have records.

One line: LinkedIn finds the people, WhatsApp closes the deals, Gmail holds the pipeline, Wise/PayPal brings in the money. Four stages connected end to end — that's a complete acquisition line.

## 13. Appendix: one-page cheat sheet

**Preconditions**

The China edition is shut down. International linkedin.com only, and it needs a stable network environment.

**Registration and profile**

Overseas email → two-step verification on → white-background photo → headline as "product + audience + region" → About section with specific products and certifications.

**Adding pace (experience values)**

New accounts: 40–75/week, 15–20/day. Mature accounts: ~100/week (the practical ceiling for most). Connection notes under 200 characters, stating your reason.

**The free plan's four walls**

~80 profile views/day; ~250–350 searches/month; 1,000 results per page; only the last 5 profile viewers shown.

**Sales Navigator (2026)**

Core ~$119.99/month (annual from ~$89.99), 30-day trial; 40+ filters, saved-search alerts, 50 InMail credits/month (refunded on a reply within 90 days).

**SSI score**

0–100, updated daily; 70+ good, 40–70 average, below 40 poor. Four pillars: brand, find, engage, relationships.

**First aid**

Temporary restriction → 1–3 days of quiet normal use. Verification requested → submit real documents. Restricted → file an appeal. After restoration → 5–10 adds/day for the first week.

**Red lines**

No batch adding on a new account. No automation tools. No ads the moment you connect. No constant switching of devices and networks.
