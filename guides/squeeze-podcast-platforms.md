## 3. Production: a toolchain that starts at $0

(Prices below were checked on official sites in August 2026, or officially disclosed.)

### 3.1 Recording

- **Free start**: phone + a quiet room + a free recording app (Audacity, for example — open source, free)
- Remote interviews: Zencastr / Riverside (free tiers) — Spotify for Creators now has Riverside recording and editing built in
- Upgrade path: a USB microphone (e.g. Samson Q2U / Shure MV7 — check official prices)

### 3.2 Editing

| Tool | Price | Core features |
|---|---|---|
| **Descript** | Free 1h/mo; Creator $24/mo | Edit audio/video like a Word doc, filler-word removal, AI voiceover, one-click Clips |
| **Audacity** | Completely free, open source | Classic audio editing |
| **ffmpeg** | Completely free, open source | Batch transcoding/editing/scripting (automation pipelines) |
| **CapCut** | Free; Pro $9.99/mo | Video podcast editing + captions |

### 3.3 AI assistance (the OPC lever)

- **TTS voiceover**: edge-tts (free) / ElevenLabs (free 10K credits/mo) — one person can run a fully voiced show
- **Transcription / captions**: Whisper (open source, free) / Descript auto-transcription
- **AI cover art**: Canva (free) templates

### 3.4 The $0 full pipeline

**Audacity / CapCut (JianYing) + Whisper + edge-tts + free Canva + phone recording = a working $0 pipeline** (the OPC spirit: one person, zero cost, go)

### 3.5 Recording in practice: your first episode, step by step

**Step 1: the room (10 minutes)**
- Find the smallest room (a closet full of clothes = natural sound absorption)
- Phone standing on the desk, 15–20cm from your mouth; put a towel underneath to kill vibration
- Turn off the AC / fan / fridge; put the phone in airplane mode (no notification sounds)
- Record a 30-second test line before starting, play it back, check the noise floor — too noisy, move

**Step 2: the script (not a word-for-word script)**
- Write an **outline + key data** (3–5 points per episode), not a full script — scripted podcasts sound scripted
- First 30 seconds of every episode: say exactly what problem this episode solves (listeners decide whether to stay within 5 seconds)
- Plant a hook every 3–5 minutes ("later there's a more counterintuitive one")

**Step 3: recording (20–40 minutes)**
- Record one take per point; if you mess up a sentence, just redo that sentence (saves editing)
- Slow down for data and numbers (listeners can't remember them; slow = memorable)
- Back up the raw file immediately when done (cloud + local, two copies)

**Step 4: editing (15–30 minutes)**
- Audacity: cut silences and mistakes, compressor to level out volume
- Descript (free 1h/mo): text-based editing — delete the words, delete the audio; one-click filler removal
- Export: MP3 at 128–192kbps (fine for podcasting, small files load fast)

**Step 5: packaging (10 minutes)**
- Cover art 3000x3000 (Apple minimum is 1400x1400; over 1.5MB gets rejected)
- Title and description written per the SEO rules in Chapter 6
- Chapters: segment titles so listeners can skip around (Spotify and Apple both support them)

**OPC note**: the full pipeline takes 2–3 hours the first time, and **1 hour per episode once you're fluent** — that's one-person-company production speed.

## 4. Hosting and distribution: RSS is the foundation

### 4.1 Choosing a host

| Platform | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Spotify for Creators** (formerly Anchor) | **Free** | official free hosting + automatic ads + subscriptions + video podcasts |
| **Libsyn** | Paid (from about $5–20/mo) | the veteran; $112M+ paid to creators cumulatively |
| **Buzzsprout** | Free tier + paid | easy to use, clear stats |
| **Self-hosted (GitHub Pages)** | **$0** | the advanced play: generate your own RSS, total control (we've run it end to end: audio push → gen_rss.py builds the feed → GitHub Pages → permanent redirect in Spotify) |

### 4.2 How distribution works (the key insight)

**Submit RSS once = everything syncs automatically:**
- Host submits the RSS once → automatically listed across Apple / Spotify / Amazon and the rest of the directories
- Apple validates submissions via RSS (Apple Podcasts Connect); Delegated Delivery lets hosts handle distribution for you
- **RSS is the moat**: switch platforms without switching your content source — listeners follow the RSS

### 4.3 Our playbook (field-tested): self-hosted RSS on GitHub, end to end

One of the least documented plays in podcasting: **cut hosting costs to $0 and keep your distribution lifeline completely in your own hands.**

**Architecture (verified working):**
```
GitHub repo (audio/ directory)
    ↓ push audio file (MP3)
gen_rss.py (scans the directory, generates a standards-compliant iTunes RSS feed)
    ↓
GitHub Pages serves feed.xml (https://yourusername.github.io/project/feed.xml)
    ↓
Import the RSS into Spotify for Creators → submit to Apple Podcasts → synced everywhere
```

**Key steps (every one earned through trial and error):**
1. **Repo structure**: `audio/EP1.mp3`, `EP2.mp3`… + `cover.jpg` (1400x1400) + `gen_rss.py`
2. **The RSS generator**: scans the audio directory → generates iTunes-compliant items (title / description / enclosure / duration / itunes:image) — **the enclosure URL must be a direct link** (the `/audio/xxx.mp3` path on GitHub Pages). This is the easiest thing to get wrong
3. **Cover compliance**: JPEG or PNG, 1400x1400 or larger, under 1.5MB — Apple rejects anything over
4. **Enable Pages**: repo Settings → Pages → select the main branch → get `https://account.github.io/project/`
5. **Validate the feed**: curl the feed (expect a 200) + run Apple's official feed validator to check the format (catch errors early, before a platform rejects you)
6. **Spotify permanent redirect**: paste the RSS URL in Spotify for Creators → set "permanent redirect" — from then on, pushing audio = auto-sync, **no manual uploads**

**Three things this play gets you (an angle the usual walkthroughs skip):**
- **$0 cost**: GitHub free + Pages free — cheaper than any host ($5–20/mo)
- **Full control**: the RSS is yours; switch platforms without switching lifelines (the Google Podcasts shutdown taught us: keep the lifeline in your own hands)
- **Automation**: push = publish — same thinking as CI/CD; a one-person company can run a "content pipeline"

**Honest limits**: needs basic command-line skills (git push); no host-style analytics dashboard (add third-party stats like Podtrac); not for complete beginners (start with Spotify for Creators free hosting instead).

**OPC view**: this is what one-person-company really means — **trade a little command-line skill for zero hosting bills and zero platform lock-in.**

### 4.4 Platform setup in practice: all three platforms, one pass

**A. Spotify for Creators (free, formerly Anchor)**
1. Go to creators.spotify.com → sign in with your Spotify account
2. "Create a show" → name / description / category (the right category = the right recommendation pool)
3. Upload cover art (1400x1400+, under 1.5MB)
4. Hosting, pick one:
   - **Platform hosting**: upload audio/video, the platform generates the RSS
   - **Import an existing RSS** (self-hosted or another host): paste the feed URL → the platform pulls it
5. Turn on monetization: accept the "Shorts Monetization Module" (if you make Shorts)
6. Check the Spotify app for your show name to confirm it's visible

**B. Apple Podcasts Connect (70.8% of downloads — a must)**
1. Go to podcastsconnect.apple.com → sign in with your Apple ID
2. "Add a show" → paste the RSS URL → the platform validates the feed
3. Review usually takes 24–48 hours; a malformed feed gets rejected (run the official feed validator first)
4. Once approved, claim the show → set up a Channel (a hub page for multiple shows)
5. Turn on subscriptions (ad-free / early access / exclusive episodes) → set pricing

**C. YouTube (#1 listening platform + the video trend)**
1. YouTube Studio with your Google account → create a channel
2. Video podcast: upload the MP4 (see Chapter 8) → set up a "Podcast" playlist
3. Audio-only show: make audiogram waveform videos (30–60 second clips) and post them as Shorts for traffic
4. Put RSS / Spotify links in the description (closes the traffic loop)

**D. Everything else (Amazon Music / Overcast etc.)**
- Auto-submit via RSS: most support "paste the feed = you're listed"; a one-time setup

**OPC note**: setting up A + B + C takes about an hour once; after that every episode is one job — **upload/push the audio, everything else syncs itself.**

## 5. Growth: 8 moves from zero to breakout

### 5.1 Cold start (Spotify official, "first 100 plays")
- Go narrow on your audience first; channels come second
- Content rule of three: useful / interesting / substantive
- Seed 30–60 second clips on social + link to the full episode
- Ask 10 friends and family to share; "ship first, perfect later"

### 5.2 Short-video clips (Spotify Clips, 2025)
- One clip per episode; surfaced on the show page / feeds / Browse / Now Playing (100+ markets)
- **Trailer-style clips convert best; hook people in the first 3–7 seconds; putting the guest's credentials up front is a trust hook**

### 5.3 Cross-platform social distribution
- **42% of listeners discover new podcasts through Instagram / TikTok / Facebook** (Spotify Fan Study)
- Audio-only shows make audiograms (30–60 second waveform videos); platforms weight video content

### 5.4 Guest strategy (Spotify official)
- Write your "ideal guest list" first (guests should match 5 of 7 traits)
- Start with people you know / peer podcasts; celebrities via DM / email cold outreach
- **Your podcast trailer is your business card**: the email carries the value proposition + audience numbers + "logistics fully handled"

### 5.5 The cross-promotion ladder (the king of sharing)
shoutout → promo swap (30-second mid-roll) → ad swap → **feed drop (swapping full episodes — the strongest move)** → guest swap → co-hosting
Case: Twenty Thousand Hertz went from a few hundred plays to millions after a recommendation from 99% Invisible

### 5.6 Series and collections
- Apple Channel: collect multiple shows / seasons on one page + channel subscriptions
- Spotify "Best place to start": steer new listeners to your strongest episode
- YouTube: series + playlists keep people watching the next one

### 5.7 Publishing patterns
- Weekdays see more publishing than weekends; US listening peaks 10:00–14:00 (26%)
- Episode length: 32% run 20–40 minutes; frequency: 33% publish every 3–7 days

### 5.8 The survival reality
Only about 500K of the 4.71M shows are active (90% dormant) — **outlasting everyone is the biggest differentiation there is** (most competitors quit within three months)

## 6. Podcast SEO: the traffic door 90% of people ignore

### 6.1 The battlefield changed (Spotify official, 2026-08)
Podcast SEO now means **Google + Spotify + AI answer engines** (more and more people "ask AI for podcast recommendations")

### 6.2 What to actually do
- **Show name: 3–4 words, unique, containing a keyword**
- **The first 250 characters of your description make or break you** (search and recommendations both read them)
- **Write episode titles in the words people actually search** (example: "am i too old to have acne" beats "Ep 47 Q&A" a hundred times over)
- Write the description after you finish recording (content first, summary second)

### 6.3 Transcript SEO (the big 2025 update)
- **Spotify auto-generates transcripts / chapters / guest-and-topic tags for every episode** (2025-09) — content is indexed at keyword level, and chapters can be recommended on their own
- **Apple has transcribed 100M+ episodes** (2025-03, in 13 languages)
- What it means: **what you say is now searchable** — keywords that occur naturally in speech (not stuffed in) are free SEO

### 6.4 Where discovery comes from
30% of new listeners via web search, 19% via friends and family, 17% via social media — **SEO + word of mouth are the two engines** (Buzzsprout 2024)

## 9. Pitfall checklist: 10 mistakes to skip

### 9.1 Platform risks
1. **Don't bet on a single platform**: Google Podcasts shut down overnight (announced 2023-09 → closed 2024-04) — **owning your RSS is the moat**; your content source follows your RSS, and platforms are just distribution channels
2. **Don't skip RSS compliance**: Apple validates submissions via RSS; a malformed feed (missing required tags / wrong cover dimensions) gets rejected — test with your host or the official validator first
3. **Don't buy subscribers or inflate plays**: platforms filter fake traffic; inflated numbers can hurt your recommendation weight or get you taken down (consistent policy across platforms)

### 9.2 Content risks
4. **Copyrighted music / assets**: use platform music libraries (Spotify's built-in one) or original / free-licensed material; podcast music infringement gets takedowns and claims too
5. **Don't wait for perfect before publishing**: "ship first, perfect later" (Spotify official) — 90% of competitors quit within three months; consistency is the biggest differentiator
6. **Don't skip transcripts**: with Spotify / Apple auto-transcription, your spoken content is keyword-searchable — natural keywords in speech are free SEO, but **deliberately stacking keyword phrases hurts the listening experience**

### 9.3 Data risks
7. **Stop quoting "66.5% die before episode 8"**: no authoritative source; the reliable figure is "roughly 90% dormant, about 500K active" (Podcast Index 2026-08)
8. **Don't write podcast CPM/RPM from memory**: industry medians (Magellan benchmark) need verification; unverified = label it "to be verified", never invent (this guide follows the same rule)
9. **Date your old data**: TikTok numbers (2023), catalog share (2023-02), and other historical figures need the year attached when cited, so nobody is misled

### 9.4 OPC special note
10. **Don't carry the whole pipeline alone**: recording / editing / distribution / growth are four workflows — automate the repetitive parts with AI tools (TTS / transcription / clipping) and keep humans for content decisions (the OPC thesis: one person + tools = one company)

## 9.5 FAQ

**Q1: No microphone — can I record on a phone?**
Yes. Phone + a quiet room + 15–20cm from your mouth is the best possible start for episode one. Listeners care about content, not audiophile sound. Upgrade path: phone → USB mic (~$50–100) → audio interface + dynamic mic.

**Q2: How long until a podcast makes money?**
No standard answer, but the data helps: 90% of shows go dormant (consistency is your opening); ad revenue is $2B+ and growing; 72% of long-time listeners have bought a recommended product. **Do 20 episodes, get RSS distribution working, and build a regular audience before you talk revenue** — revenue is a byproduct of content.

**Q3: Do I need both audio and video?**
In 2026: **yes**. 57% of listeners both listen and watch; 72% prefer video. It's not double the work: record video once → audio to podcast platforms + video to YouTube; one piece of content, two traffic streams.

**Q4: Will listeners hate AI voiceover?**
It depends: for tutorials and news (high information density) AI voice is acceptable; interviews and emotional shows need a human feel. Recommendation: **OPC beginners can use TTS, but position the show as "tool / information," not "companionship"** — and use a real human voice whenever you have one.

**Q5: How often should I publish?**
Data: 33% of shows publish every 3–7 days, 40% every 8–14 days. **For a one-person company: quality over frequency** (platforms don't punish breaks); consistency beats volume — one episode a week without fail beats three a week that fizzle out.

**Q6: How do I know if my podcast is doing well?**
Three metrics: ① per-episode download / play trend (rising = on track) ② completion rate (how many people finish) ③ active listener behavior (comments / shares / DMs). **Don't watch whether a single episode blows up; watch trends and retention.**

**Q7: Do I need a website?**
Not required, but recommended: a simple show page (show intro + RSS link + contact info) is your podcast's business card — platform visitors can settle on your own turf. Build one free on GitHub Pages / Notion.

- Projection: **AI answer engines become a new podcast traffic door** — shows with clean transcripts and clear descriptions get recommended first
- Action: make the first 250 characters of your description say exactly "what problem this show solves," so an AI can understand it at a glance

- Facts: the YouTube YPP threshold doubles in 2027-02 (podcast channels on YouTube feel it too); 90% of podcasts go dormant
- Projection: **higher bars flush out amateurs and keep professionals** — people who enter in 2026 hold a first-mover edge once the bar doubles in 2027
- Action: start now; make "survive one year" the minimum goal (90% of competitors quit within three months)

**Trend 4: the one-person company (OPC) model goes mainstream**
- Facts: the AI toolchain is mature (TTS / transcription / clipping / auto-distribution, all available at $0)
- Projection: **marginal production cost approaches zero** — "one person, one podcast" flips from exception to norm; competition shifts from "who can endure" to "who has better content"
